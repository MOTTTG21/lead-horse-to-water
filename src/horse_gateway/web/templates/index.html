How to play

You're visiting a horse that hasn't had water in a while. Your goal: get it to drink. It won't be easy, and it won't be quick.

Two things have to be true at once

The horse has to genuinely want to. That only happens through real conversation -- listen to what it says, reflect its feelings back, ask open questions, be patient. Being pushy, confrontational, or demanding does not work, and can set you back.

The water has to actually be there. Use the barn-upkeep actions (by button or by just saying what you want to do) to build up its thirst and then make water available again, and give it a little time to settle.

Both matter. A thirsty horse with no trust has no reason to drink. A horse that trusts you completely still won't drink from an empty trough.

The horse will make excuses

It won't just say no and stop talking. It has real, specific reasons it's holding back, and those reasons shift as trust grows -- from guarded and deflecting, to more personal, to something close to genuine vulnerability. Being nice in general isn't enough; you have to actually engage with whatever it just told you.

Watch the meters

The Trust bar reflects how much the horse trusts you -- if it hits zero, the horse disengages and the session ends. The Stage label tracks how close it is to genuinely considering drinking.

You can act by typing or by clicking

Every action below the chat can also just be said in plain words -- "I'll clean the trough," "you decide," even a bare "Graze." The buttons are there so nothing is hidden; use whichever feels natural.

When it ends

A win or a loss ends the attempt and shows a debrief. Closing it starts a fresh attempt -- your progress in the Field Guide and the things you've discovered carry over, so it's worth trying more than once.
